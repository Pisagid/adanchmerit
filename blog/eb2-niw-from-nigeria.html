Can I apply for EB-2 NIW from Nigeria?

Updated 16 November 2026 · Adanch Merit Consulting

Short answer: yes. You can file an EB-2 National Interest Waiver petition from Lagos, Abuja, Enugu or anywhere in Nigeria. You don't need a US employer, a job offer or a US address. If USCIS approves your petition, you finish the process at the US Embassy in Abuja or the Consulate in Lagos. This is called consular processing.

Who the EB-2 NIW is for

The EB-2 NIW is for professionals with an advanced degree (a Master's or PhD, or a Bachelor's plus five years of progressive experience) or exceptional ability in their field. On top of that, you must satisfy the three-part Matter of Dhanasar test:

  • Your work has substantial merit and national importance to the United States, not just to one employer.
  • You are well placed to advance it, shown by your education, track record, results and plans.
  • On balance, it helps the US to waive the job offer and labour certification for you.

Many Nigerian engineers, doctors, pharmacists, researchers, data scientists, founders and public-health workers have records that can fit this test, if the evidence is organised well.

The steps, from Nigeria to the US

1. Build your evidence and file Form I-140

Your petition package includes Form I-140, a petition letter that argues each Dhanasar prong, recommendation letters, and evidence such as publications, citations, projects, awards, media and a plan of what you'll do in the US. The government filing fee for a self-petitioner is $715 for the I-140 ($665 if filed online) plus a $300 Asylum Program Fee, so $1,015 in total. Premium processing costs $2,965 and gets a decision in 45 business days.

2. Wait for USCIS's decision

Approval rates have dropped. USCIS's own data shows EB-2 NIW approvals fell from 95.7% in FY2022 to 55.2% in FY2025, and quarterly rates in FY2026 have run between about 43% and 55%. Since August 2026, USCIS has also been able to deny weak petitions without first sending a Request for Evidence. A complete, well-argued file from day one matters more than ever.

3. National Visa Center (NVC)

After approval, USCIS sends your approved petition to the National Visa Center. The NVC collects each applicant's fee, DS-260 and civil documents.

Each person (you, your spouse and each child) completes a Form DS-260 online and pays the $345 employment-based immigrant visa fee. You'll upload documents like birth certificates, marriage certificate, passports and police certificates.

4. Wait for your priority date

Your priority date is the day USCIS received your I-140. You can only get your visa when that date is earlier than the "Final Action Date" for EB-2 Rest of World, which is the column that covers Nigeria.

In the November 2026 Visa Bulletin, the EB-2 Final Action Date for Rest of World advanced to 1 April 2025. So anyone who filed before that date can be scheduled now, and later filers wait. EB-1, by contrast, is "Current" for Rest of World, which is one reason strong candidates also look at EB-1A.

5. Medical exam and interview

Each applicant does a medical exam with an embassy-approved panel physician, usually costing around $200 to $500 per person. Then you attend the immigrant visa interview at the US Embassy in Abuja or the US Consulate General in Lagos, depending on where your case is assigned.

6. Pay the USCIS Immigrant Fee and travel

After the visa is issued, each person pays the $235 USCIS Immigrant Fee, which pays for the physical green card. You enter the US on your immigrant visa, and you become a permanent resident on the day you're admitted. The card is mailed to your US address.

Common mistakes Nigerian applicants make

  • Writing about the job, not the national importance. "I'm a good engineer" isn't enough. Explain why your specific work matters to the US.
  • Generic recommendation letters. Letters should come from independent experts and describe specific impact, with examples.
  • Weak proposed endeavour. USCIS wants a clear, specific plan for what you'll do in the US.
  • Document gaps. Missing birth certificates, name differences across documents or late police certificates cause NVC delays. Start gathering early.
  • Paying for promises. Nobody can guarantee approval. Be careful of anyone who does.
